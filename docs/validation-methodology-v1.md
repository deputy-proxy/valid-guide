# Valid.guide Validation Methodology v1

> **Status:** Methodology proposal for implementation
> **Version:** 1.0
> **Date:** 2026-09-08
> **Owner:** Valid.guide
> **Scope:** Courses, cohort courses, guides, ebook-based learning products, workshops, programs, memberships, and other approved learning products

## 1. Purpose

Valid.guide evaluates whether an online learning product demonstrates sufficient quality, coherence, credibility, usefulness, and delivery integrity to earn the Valid.guide Validation.

The methodology evaluates the **product**, not the creator's reputation and not whether an individual learner is guaranteed to achieve the promised outcome.

The central question is:

> **Does the product, as actually delivered, provide credible evidence that it deserves the trust implied by its stated promise?**

Validation is therefore an evidence-based professional judgement constrained by a published standard, not a popularity score and not a customer-satisfaction survey.

---

## 2. Methodological principles

1. **Independence** – commercial payment never determines the outcome.
2. **Evidence before assertion** – significant findings must be grounded in observable evidence or explicitly identified professional judgement.
3. **Product over personality** – creator fame, credentials, audience size, sales and social proof are not substitutes for product quality.
4. **Promise-relative evaluation** – quality is assessed against what the product claims to provide to its stated audience.
5. **Outcome realism** – ambitious claims require proportionally strong support.
6. **Context sensitivity** – standards differ by product type, subject, audience and complexity.
7. **Fairness** – products are evaluated on information reasonably available to the creator and Auditor at the time of evaluation.
8. **Transparency** – criteria, scoring logic and the meaning of validation are public.
9. **Reproducibility** – another qualified Auditor should be able to understand why a conclusion was reached from the recorded evidence and rationale.
10. **No false precision** – numerical scores summarize structured judgement; they do not pretend that educational quality is a laboratory measurement.
11. **Historical integrity** – the standard and product release evaluated are permanently recorded.
12. **Continuous improvement** – methodology revisions create new versions and never rewrite historical evaluations.

---

## 3. Scope of evaluation

The unit of evaluation is a specific **Product Release**: the identifiable edition/version/publication state actually examined by Valid.guide.

The evaluation may consider:

- product structure and curriculum
- instructional/content material
- stated audience
- stated learning outcomes
- delivery model
- exercises and activities
- assessment mechanisms
- supporting evidence and references
- usability and accessibility
- practical applicability
- product-level claims and marketing promises where they materially affect buyer expectations
- observed learner experience where access permits it

The evaluation does not normally certify:

- business profitability
- creator popularity
- legal compliance in every jurisdiction
- individual learner outcomes
- future product performance
- customer-service quality unrelated to the learning experience
- pricing as an independent quality measure

Commercial price may be recorded as contextual metadata, but **price must not alter a product's intrinsic quality assessment**. Value-for-money is considered only where explicitly defined by the applicable product-type standard.

---

## 4. Evaluation architecture

The methodology uses a **common core + product-type modules** model.

Every product is assessed through the common core. Product-specific criteria are then added according to its format.

This avoids two bad extremes:

- treating a four-week cohort, a self-paced ebook and a membership community as identical products;
- creating seven unrelated methodologies that eventually disagree about what “quality” means.

### Product-type modules

| Product type | Additional emphasis |
|---|---|
| Course | instructional design, sequencing, practice, assessment, learner progression |
| Cohort course | live facilitation, interaction, feedback, cohort mechanics, progression |
| Guide | clarity, structure, usability, actionable guidance, evidence |
| Ebook learning product | instructional organization, examples, exercises, transfer to practice |
| Workshop | learning objective, activity design, facilitation, practice, feedback |
| Program | architecture across components, progression, integration, sustained application |
| Membership | continuing value, content cadence, community/interaction, ongoing learning pathway |
| Other | Admin selects and documents an appropriate applicability profile before evaluation |

## 5. Core evaluation dimensions

The initial standard contains **10 dimensions**. Each dimension is assessed through criteria and subcriteria appropriate to the product type.

### D1. Promise & Audience Fit

**Question:** Is it clear what the product is for, who it is for, and what it credibly promises?

Subcriteria:

- audience is identifiable
- learner problem/need is clear
- scope is understandable
- learning promise is specific enough to evaluate
- claims are proportionate to the product's actual scope
- exclusions/limitations are not materially misleading

### D2. Subject-Matter Credibility

**Question:** Is the knowledge presented sufficiently accurate, current, relevant and credible for the stated purpose?

Subcriteria:

- factual accuracy
- conceptual soundness
- appropriate depth
- currency where the subject changes materially
- source quality where sourcing is expected
- creator/expert competence where relevant
- distinction between evidence, opinion and recommendation

### D3. Content Quality & Completeness

**Question:** Does the delivered content adequately cover what the product promises?

Subcriteria:

- relevance
- accuracy
- completeness relative to stated scope
- depth appropriate to audience
- examples and explanations
- unnecessary material is controlled
- important gaps are identified

### D4. Structure & Coherence

**Question:** Is the learning experience logically organized and internally coherent?

Subcriteria:

- logical sequencing
- clear progression
- consistent terminology
- navigability
- relationships between components are understandable
- prerequisites are stated where needed
- no material contradictions

### D5. Learning Design & Engagement

**Question:** Does the product use an appropriate learning design rather than merely presenting information?

Subcriteria:

- active learning where appropriate
- meaningful practice
- retrieval/application opportunities
- feedback mechanisms
- appropriate pacing
- interaction appropriate to format
- assessment or self-check mechanisms where needed

The weight of this dimension is reduced for products whose explicit purpose is reference rather than instruction.

### D6. Practical Applicability & Transfer

**Question:** Can the learner reasonably use what is taught or provided?

Subcriteria:

- actionable guidance
- realistic examples
- practice or application
- transfer to relevant contexts
- tools/templates/checklists where appropriate
- connection between knowledge and action

### D7. Evidence, Support & Intellectual Integrity

**Question:** Are important claims and recommendations adequately supported?

Subcriteria:

- credible references where required
- evidence proportional to claim strength
- transparent sourcing
- distinction between established knowledge and personal opinion
- avoidance of fabricated authority
- appropriate citation/attribution

### D8. Usability, Accessibility & Delivery Integrity

**Question:** Can the intended audience reasonably access and use the product as represented?

Subcriteria:

- technical accessibility
- navigation
- readability
- media quality
- instructions
- accessibility considerations appropriate to format
- delivered components match stated components
- links/materials function sufficiently for evaluation

### D9. Differentiation & Added Value

**Question:** Does the product provide a meaningful reason to exist beyond repackaging generic information?

Subcriteria:

- distinctive approach, synthesis or application
- useful original material where claimed
- meaningful organization or curation
- contextual value
- clear reason the product may be useful to its intended audience

Originality is **not** required in the sense of inventing new knowledge. A product may earn this criterion through exceptional curation, synthesis, pedagogy, application or usability.

### D10. Outcome Realism & Overall Product Integrity

**Question:** Do the product's promised outcomes, delivery mechanism and limitations fit together credibly?

Subcriteria:

- outcomes are achievable in principle through the product
- required learner effort is realistic
- prerequisites are disclosed
- limitations are represented honestly
- no material mismatch between marketing promise and delivered experience
- the overall product is internally trustworthy

## 6. Product-type weighting

Weights are applied at the **criterion level**, not by arbitrarily changing the meaning of the core dimensions.

Recommended starting profiles:

| Dimension | Course | Cohort | Guide | Ebook | Workshop | Program | Membership |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D1 Promise & Audience | 10% | 10% | 12% | 12% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| D2 Subject Credibility | 12% | 12% | 14% | 14% | 12% | 12% | 12% |
| D3 Content Quality | 14% | 12% | 16% | 16% | 10% | 12% | 12% |
| D4 Structure & Coherence | 10% | 8% | 12% | 12% | 8% | 10% | 8% |
| D5 Learning Design | 16% | 18% | 8% | 8% | 18% | 14% | 10% |
| D6 Practical Transfer | 12% | 12% | 12% | 12% | 12% | 12% | 10% |
| D7 Evidence & Integrity | 8% | 8% | 10% | 10% | 8% | 8% | 8% |
| D8 Usability & Delivery | 6% | 8% | 6% | 6% | 6% | 6% | 8% |
| D9 Differentiation | 5% | 4% | 5% | 4% | 5% | 5% | 5% |
| D10 Outcome Realism & Integrity | 7% | 8% | 5% | 6% | 11% | 11% | 17% |
| **Total** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** |

These are **starting weights**, not immutable truth. They should be stored in the methodology database and revised only through a new Standard Version.

For memberships, D10 is intentionally stronger because the product's continuing promise and ongoing value are central to whether validation remains meaningful.

## 7. Criterion assessment scale

Each applicable criterion receives two related outputs:

### 7.1 Categorical assessment

- **Exceeds** – clearly stronger than the standard expected for the product type.
- **Meets** – satisfies the published standard.
- **Partially Meets** – meaningful deficiency exists, but the criterion is substantially addressed.
- **Does Not Meet** – material deficiency prevents the criterion from satisfying the standard.
- **Insufficient Evidence** – the Auditor cannot reasonably determine performance from available evidence.
- **Not Applicable** – criterion genuinely does not apply to the product.

### 7.2 Numerical score

Where scoring is enabled, the categorical result maps to a 0–100 score using methodology-defined anchors.

Recommended anchors:

- Exceeds: 90–100
- Meets: 75–89
- Partially Meets: 50–74
- Does Not Meet: 0–49
- Insufficient Evidence: no score
- Not Applicable: excluded from denominator

The exact number is secondary to the evidence and categorical judgement.

---

## 8. Evidence requirements

Auditors must distinguish between:

1. **Observed evidence** – directly inspected product material or behavior.
2. **Creator-supplied evidence** – documents, references, credentials or explanations supplied during intake.
3. **External evidence** – sources outside the product, used only where the applicable criterion permits it.
4. **Professional judgement** – reasoned expert interpretation where a criterion cannot be reduced to a factual observation.

### Evidence sufficiency

A criterion should not receive a confident positive assessment when its critical claims cannot reasonably be examined.

If evidence is insufficient because the creator failed to provide required access/materials, the Auditor should record **Insufficient Evidence**, not infer quality.

Repeated inability to provide essential evidence may itself become a finding concerning delivery integrity or transparency.

---

## 9. Mandatory criteria and disqualifiers

Not every criterion is equally mandatory.

### 9.1 Universal non-negotiables

The following are presumptive validation blockers when material:

1. **Materially deceptive claims** about what the product contains or can achieve.
2. **Material factual unreliability** in subject matter where accuracy is essential.
3. **Critical safety or professional-risk defects** where the product gives consequential advice without adequate safeguards.
4. **Material plagiarism, fabricated sources or fabricated evidence** discovered within scope.
5. **Material mismatch** between the product represented for evaluation and the product actually delivered.
6. **Essential component absent** where that component is necessary for the stated learning promise.
7. **Systematic evidence failure** where a product's central claims cannot be substantiated and the methodology requires substantiation.

These are not automatic accusations. The finding must be documented, evidence-based and proportionate.

### 9.2 Mandatory criterion rule

Each Standard Version identifies a set of mandatory criteria.

A validation cannot pass if a mandatory criterion is **Does Not Meet**.

A mandatory criterion may be **Insufficient Evidence** only when the evidence gap is not attributable to a material omission by the creator and the criterion can legitimately remain unresolved. Otherwise, unresolved critical evidence prevents validation.

---

## 10. Validation decision logic

Validation is a **gated weighted decision**, not a simple average.

A product is **VALIDATED** only when all of the following are true:

1. All applicable mandatory criteria meet at least the required threshold.
2. No active disqualifying finding exists.
3. The weighted score meets the minimum overall threshold.
4. No core dimension falls below its minimum dimension threshold.
5. Evidence coverage is sufficient for the product's central claims.
6. The Auditor(s) conclude that the product is coherent with its stated audience and promise.
7. Where multiple Auditors are assigned, the decision is consistent with the applicable majority-vote rules.

### Recommended thresholds

- Overall weighted score: **≥ 75/100**
- Mandatory criteria: **≥ 75/100 / Meets**
- Core dimension floor: **≥ 60/100**, unless the product-type profile explicitly makes that dimension non-core
- No material disqualifier

A product may therefore pass with weaknesses. A 76/100 product with a few non-critical weaknesses can be validated. A 90/100 product with a critical mandatory failure cannot.

This is deliberate. Otherwise the methodology becomes a mathematical loophole generator, which is a very human way to avoid making an actual judgement.

---

## 11. Multiple Auditors

The number of Auditors is determined by evaluation complexity and is always odd.

Recommended default:

- **Simple:** 1 Auditor
- **Standard:** 1 Auditor, with second-review capability
- **Complex:** 3 Auditors
- **Exceptional/high-risk:** 5 Auditors where Admin determines that the additional independence is justified

All assigned Auditors evaluate independently before comparison.

There is no lead Auditor.

### Voting

Where the methodology designates a criterion for collective determination, each Auditor's submitted Criterion Result becomes one vote.

- Simple majority determines the criterion result.
- Minority positions remain immutable in the historical record.
- Votes never overwrite the underlying Auditor assessments.

Administrative intervention can affect the **final Evaluation Decision**, but cannot modify an Auditor's original result.

---

## 12. Auditor judgement and confidence

Auditors must provide rationale for material positive or negative findings.

Confidence may be recorded separately:

- High
- Medium
- Low

Confidence is not a quality score. It expresses how strongly the available evidence supports the Auditor's conclusion.

Low-confidence findings should normally trigger either additional evidence collection or cautious wording in the report.

---

## 13. Auditor competence requirements

Auditor eligibility is topic-specific.

For each evaluation, Valid.guide should verify:

1. Demonstrated subject-matter expertise.
2. Relevant teaching, training or creator experience where applicable to the product.
3. Ability to evaluate the relevant product format.
4. Methodological literacy sufficient to apply the standard.
5. Appropriate language competence.

Credentials are useful evidence but are not universally mandatory.

A directly competing commercial product is a **potential conflict**, not an automatic disqualification. The Auditor must disclose it and Valid.guide Admin decides.

An Auditor cannot evaluate a product in which they previously participated.

COI declarations are required annually and for every assignment.

---

## 14. Complexity classification

Complexity determines the depth of evaluation and Auditor count. It is not a quality rating.

### Simple

Typical characteristics:

- limited scope
- single-format delivery
- relatively small amount of material
- straightforward claims
- low interaction/dependency between components
- low consequence if misunderstood

### Standard

Typical characteristics:

- meaningful instructional structure
- multiple modules/components
- substantive learning claims
- exercises or assessments
- moderate amount of material

### Complex

Typical characteristics:

- multiple interconnected components
- cohort/live elements
- significant assessment or feedback systems
- substantial duration or volume
- multiple delivery mechanisms
- complex outcome claims
- substantial evidence requirements

### Exceptional

Reserved for products where complexity, risk, novelty or consequence makes additional independent review materially valuable.

Complexity is assigned by Valid.guide Admin **before payment** and determines the quoted price.

---

## 15. Product changes after validation

Validation attaches to the evaluated Product Release, not indefinitely to every future version of the product.

Material changes include:

- new edition
- new version
- new publication/release state
- material content changes
- material learning-design changes
- material scope or audience changes
- material outcome/promise changes
- product rename/rebrand where it changes product identity
- material changes to quantitative metadata used to identify the validated release

Pricing changes, instructor changes and platform migration do not automatically require revalidation unless they produce a material change under another criterion.

Creators must notify Valid.guide of material changes.

An unannounced material change may cause temporary suspension pending review and, where confirmed, permanent revocation of the validation.

If an old validated release remains genuinely available in parallel with a newer release, the old validation may remain associated with that specific release. Public verification must make the distinction unmistakable.

## 16. Report structure

The creator report contains:

1. Executive summary
2. Product and release identity
3. Evaluation scope
4. Standard and version
5. Methodology explanation
6. Overall result
7. Overall score
8. Dimension-level results
9. Criterion-level results
10. Strengths
11. Weaknesses
12. Risks
13. Evidence and rationale
14. Recommendations
15. Auditor information according to disclosure policy
16. Decision rationale
17. Revision history

The public report is a controlled summary. By default it exposes:

- overall result
- criterion-level results
- scores/ratings
- strengths
- weaknesses

The creator may enable publication of the full report on the validation page.

The public abstract is produced from the Valid.guide report, not supplied as creator-written marketing copy.

---

## 17. Clarification and appeal

Creators may request:

- clarification of a finding
- correction of a factual error

A formal review/appeal is available only on defined grounds:

- procedural error
- material factual error
- conflict-of-interest concern
- demonstrably flawed application of the published methodology

Creators may not use the process to negotiate a different judgement simply because they dislike the result.

Disputes are reviewed by Auditor(s) who did not participate in the initial evaluation.

If the review establishes that the initial evaluation was materially flawed, a new evaluation may be commissioned. The original record remains immutable and the new evaluation establishes its own historical decision/validation state.

---

## 18. Public interpretation of the score

The public score is secondary to the validation status.

Recommended public presentation:

- **Validated** – meets the Valid.guide standard.
- **Not Validated** – does not currently meet the Valid.guide standard.

The score should be accompanied by dimension results and explanatory findings.

Valid.guide must never present the score as:

- a probability of learner success
- a guarantee of outcomes
- a ranking of all products
- an objective scientific measurement of educational quality

---

## 19. Methodology governance

The methodology is stored as a versioned Standard Version.

Admins:

- create and edit draft versions
- approve versions
- announce future effective dates
- retire older versions

Rule:

`effective standard at evaluation start → frozen for evaluation → future standard applies only to future evaluations`

Historical Standard Versions remain publicly accessible in full.

No published methodology version is silently edited.

---

## 20. Initial calibration protocol

Before the first public evaluation, Valid.guide should run a calibration exercise using several representative products.

Recommended calibration set:

- one strong self-paced course
- one mediocre course
- one obviously weak course
- one guide/reference product
- one cohort/live product
- one product with ambitious claims
- one product with insufficient evidence

At least two qualified Auditors should independently score the calibration cases.

The purpose is to identify:

- ambiguous criteria
- inconsistent scoring anchors
- excessive weighting
- criteria that cannot be reliably evidenced
- disagreements that the methodology does not resolve cleanly

Calibration results should inform the next Standard Version rather than being used to retroactively alter completed evaluations.

---

## 21. Quality-control metrics for Valid.guide

Valid.guide should monitor methodology performance itself.

Useful internal metrics include:

- inter-Auditor agreement by criterion
- frequency of Insufficient Evidence
- frequency of appeals and successful appeals
- frequency of validation reversal after dispute
- criterion-level variance between Auditors
- time spent per complexity level
- evidence sufficiency rates
- percentage of evaluations requiring Admin intervention
- false-positive/false-negative signals identified through later review

These metrics should improve the methodology, not become public product ratings.

---

## 22. What the methodology deliberately does not do

It does not:

- promise that validated products are the best products available
- certify that every learner will succeed
- convert learner popularity into quality
- allow creators to purchase a positive outcome
- use sales volume as evidence of instructional quality
- allow a creator to select favorable Auditors
- let an Admin silently rewrite Auditor assessments
- make a single numerical score the sole basis of validation
- pretend every learning format can be judged identically

## 24. Final validation rule

The Valid.guide Validation is awarded when:

> **A qualified and conflict-cleared Auditor group, applying the published Standard Version to the identified Product Release, finds sufficient evidence that the product meets the mandatory requirements, clears all material disqualifiers, satisfies the minimum overall and dimension-level thresholds, and is coherent with its stated audience, promise and delivery.**

The badge communicates that conclusion. It does not communicate a guarantee about the future, the creator, or an individual learner.
